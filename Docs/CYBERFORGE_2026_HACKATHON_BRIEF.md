## Challenge scope and acceptable directions

The challenge is **AI in Cybersecurity**: build something useful and demonstrable that improves how an organization detects threats, responds to incidents, or manages cyber risk. The listed areas are examples, **not fixed problem statements**:

- Threat detection: suspicious behavior, anomalies, emerging threats.
- Phishing detection, including AI-generated social engineering.
- AI-assisted security operations: alert analysis, prioritization, response workflows.
- Vulnerability detection and secure-coding assistance.
- AI security: prompt-injection detection, misuse prevention, LLM security.
- Compliance automation and evidence collection.
- Fraud detection: suspicious transactions, account takeover, bots.
- Security awareness and training.

Teams may propose another cybersecurity problem. The event emphasizes a **working product/prototype** and a **live demonstration**, so an idea-only slide deck is insufficient for the offline stage. [Official theme and overview](https://hackculture.io/hackathons/cyberforge-hackathon-2026)

## Judging: what is known, and what it implies

The official list is **technical execution, innovation, cybersecurity relevance, usability, and overall impact**. The page publishes **no numerical weights**, scoring scale, judging panel, tie-breaker, or detailed distinction between idea-round and final-round rubrics. [Official rules](https://hackculture.io/hackathons/cyberforge-hackathon-2026)

For **idea-round selection**, the participant says judges have discretion, with **presentation, innovation, and impact** likely to matter. Those are plausible factors, **not a published selection rubric**. Selected teams should hear from judges 2–3 days before the in-office final. There is currently **no official AI-use rubric** or AI-use restriction, per the participant.

Practical interpretation for a competitive entry (**analysis, not additional rules**):

- **Technical execution:** show an end-to-end path that actually works, with reproducible behavior and a reliable live demo.
- **Innovation:** make the new contribution specific; explain why it improves on an ordinary rules-based tool or generic LLM wrapper.
- **Cybersecurity relevance:** define the threat, user, workflow, and permitted test environment. Show that the feature solves a security problem, rather than adding AI decoration to a dashboard.
- **Usability:** demonstrate a clear analyst or operator flow, understandable outputs, and actionable decisions.
- **Impact:** quantify an outcome where possible, such as detection quality, false positives, triage time, or analyst effort. Explain data limitations honestly.

Because the event is also a hiring opportunity for aspiring SDEs/builders, prepare to discuss architecture, implementation choices, debugging, and individual contributions. That is an inference from the event's hiring description, **not** an announced scoring criterion.
